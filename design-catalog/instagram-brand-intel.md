## The Linktree is the real front door

`linktr.ee/RosesDetroit`, tagline **"Enchanting Polish restaurant using the best of Michigan produce on Detroit's East Side."** That sentence is the hero line for the new site. It is theirs, it is accurate, and it is better than anything currently on `rosesdetroit.com`.

It also surfaces two channels the website does not know about: **Facebook** (`facebook.com/rosesfinefood`) and **Threads**.

## Operator story

- **Molly Mitchell**, chef and owner. Howell, MI native. Polish American heritage is the menu's organising idea.
- **Aaron Rush**, general manager, joined at the 2025 reopening after years as a regular. Mitchell on him: *"He orchestrates it all like a dance."*
- Rose's Fine Food opened **2014**, closed **2023**, reopened **September 2025** as Roses, dinner-only.
- Mitchell calls it **"my third business in this space."**
- She was **in Poland in late August 2026** researching, and said so publicly: *"wish I was there but I'm currently in Poland, learning about the culture and researching delicious things to bring home to detroit!!"*

## Visual system as practised

- **The mark is a bow.** The site's logo file is literally `redbow.png`, and it is a hand-drawn ribbon bow in vivid ember orange (`#F97743`, measured across 41.5k opaque pixels). It is used as the header logo and as the favicon. It is not a wordmark, it has no lockup, and at 104x50 in the Toast header it is doing all the identity work on the site alone.
- **The printed menu is the actual brand surface.** Sage ground `#B3CDB4`, crimson ink `#9C1B22`, single-colour line illustration throughout.
- **The illustration style is Polish papercut folk art** (*wycinanki*): a rosette-style rose, wheat sheaves, a peacock among foliage, a woman carrying a basket of bread, a footed bowl of fruit, and wavy rule borders. All in one ink weight. This is the motif system, and it is strong, specific and ownable. It has never appeared on the website.
- **"Witamy!"** ("Welcome!") is set on the menu next to the word Menu. The Polish is used as warmth, not as decoration.
- **Photography**, as practised on the feed: warm, candlelit, close-cropped plates on the actual mismatched crockery, plus wide room shots with flowers and people in them. Press photography for Hour Detroit was shot by **Jacob Lewkow** (assistant Nicholas Jamora) and is the highest-quality imagery that exists of the room.
- **The flowers are part of the brand.** `@the_flowering_hearth_` is credited in the bio, and the New York Times singled out the *"colorful bouquets plucked from the parking lot garden out back."*

> **Not verified:** none of the captured captions carry a photographer credit, so authorship of the feed imagery is unknown. Do not assume the restaurant owns or can relicense it. This is an owner question, logged in [`assets-needed.md`](assets-needed.md).

## Voice and copy patterns

Drawn from captions, not inferred.

**What it does:**
- First person, Molly's own voice, unedited. Lowercase "detroit". Typos left in ("I didnt realize").
- Exclamation stacks. `!!!` and `!!` are normal punctuation here.
- Emoji as punctuation, not decoration: ✨ around a dish name, ❤️‍🔥, 🤍, 💞. Sign-off "Xoxoxoxox".
- **Credits people constantly.** The NYT post spends more words thanking farms than celebrating the award: *"A big part of what makes a restaurant good is where the food is sourced we are eternally grateful to all the wonderful folks we work with."*
- Dish posts are the menu text verbatim: *"✨Kotlet✨ / organic chicken, squash mizeria, Calabrian chili crème fraiche, pickle onions, dill."* No adjectives added, no upsell.
- Self-deprecating about the hard parts: *"Messy but that's what getting back up is like."*

**What it never does:** marketing language, third-person restaurant-speak, "nestled", "elevated dining experience", calls to action. There is no "Book now!" in any captured caption.

**Implication for the site:** the copy should read as if Molly wrote it, because on every other channel she does. The current site's "Join Our Family / Community Heart / Take a peek into where the magic happens" is the exact opposite register and it will read as fake to anyone who follows the account.

## Program vocabulary

The words the brand actually uses, for copy and for menu-section naming: *Witamy*, pierogi, uszka, kluski, gołąbki, kotlet, chłodnik, kiełbasa, naleśniki, mizeria, surówka, Babcia's pickles, Pole Town Salad (a Poletown pun), Polish Nachos, Roses Burger, "Polish American", "seasonal", "Michigan produce", "Detroit grown", "diner".

Preservation and pickling run through everything: pickled red onion, dill oil, Babcia's pickles, pickled lemon, dill pickle dressing, pickle martinis (press), preserved lemon crème fraîche. **Pickling is the menu's signature technique and deserves to be named on the site.**
