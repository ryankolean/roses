Typography

EB Garamond and Montserrat.

Both are visual matches to the faces on the printed menu, not confirmed originals. EB Garamond reproduces the oldstyle serif of the dish descriptions, including the oldstyle figures. Montserrat reproduces the heavy geometric sans of the dish names and section heads. Swap either the moment the real files arrive.

The ornate Victorian face used for the word Menu on the printed menu is unidentified. Until the source file arrives it appears only as artwork inside the logo lockup, never as live text. Half-matching an ornate display face looks worse than not using one.

Divider

The wavy rule from the printed menu is the section divider across the whole site. It is the cheapest and most repeatable piece of the motif system.

Motif

Polish papercut, one ink.

The printed menu carries six wycinanki figures: a rose rosette, wheat sheaves, a peacock among foliage, a woman carrying a basket of bread, a footed bowl of fruit, and this wavy rule. All in a single crimson ink weight. It is specific, it is ownable, and nothing else in Detroit dining looks like it.

Only the wavy rule is reproduced here. The five figurative motifs are still locked inside a flattened JPEG. They must be vectorised from the designer’s source files, not traced. Tracing a papercut from a compressed image loses exactly the crispness that makes it a papercut.
