The situation

The reputation and the website are not the same restaurant.

Inside twelve months Roses has won the Detroit Free Press 2026 Restaurant of the Year, landed on the New York Times’ 50 favourite restaurants in America, taken a Detroit News three-star review and made Hour Detroit’s Best New Restaurants.

Its website is an unedited Toast Sites template wrapped around a single JPEG. It mentions none of the awards, links no reservation, and publishes facts that are wrong.

  • The menu is a picture. One JPEG, rendered 359×408 CSS px on a 390px phone, with alt text that names no dish. No indexable menu text, no Menu schema. A blind guest cannot read it at all.
  • /contact publishes the closed diner’s hours. “Open Tuesday-Sunday, 8am to 3pm. Closed Mondays.” Roses is dinner-only and closed Tuesday. It contradicts the live hours widget on the same page.
  • /our-story claims the business dates from 1964. The building does. Rose’s Fine Food opened in 2014, closed in 2023, reopened in 2025. The page invents a multi-generation family history.
  • /gallery advertises breakfast and lunch at a dinner-only restaurant, and contains no photographs.
  • /order is broken and is linked from the primary navigation.
  • No reservation path exists on the site, although the restaurant books on Resy.
  • Body text fails WCAG AA. Measured at 3.52:1. The h1 measures 2.77:1.
  • The name renders five different ways across surfaces the restaurant owns. Yelp carries two duplicate listings, both filed under Breakfast & Brunch.

The good news

The brand already exists. It is on the printed menu.

Sage #B3CDB4 and crimson #9C1B22 are pixel-sampled from the menu artwork. Ember #F97743 is sampled from the logo bow. The illustration system is Polish papercut, wycinanki: a rose rosette, wheat sheaves, a peacock, a woman carrying bread, a bowl of fruit, and the wavy rule running down this page.

None of it has ever appeared on the website.

Method

Measured, not assumed.

The live site sits behind Cloudflare’s managed challenge, which returns 403 to plain HTTP clients. That challenge belongs to Toast, not the restaurant: the apex A record resolves to an IP registered to Toast, Inc. A normal browser clears the JS challenge with no interaction, so the audit was done in a rendered session. Nothing was bypassed.

Labelled unverified where it could not be confirmed: the three fonts on the printed menu cannot be identified from a JPEG, the Detroit News review is paywalled so its sentences are not quoted, Core Web Vitals field data needs a Search Console the restaurant controls, and ownership of the Instagram photography is unknown.

Precedent: ryankolean/umbo and ryankolean/meantime. Static multi-page HTML, no build step, GitHub Pages. The one deviation here is a Markdown-sourced menu, because this menu changes and the owner has to be able to change it.
